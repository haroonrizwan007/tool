JPG and PNG are the two formats you will meet most often, and choosing the wrong one costs you either quality or bandwidth. The rule of thumb is short: **use JPG for photographs and PNG for graphics that need sharp edges or transparency.**

## How JPG works

JPG throws away detail the human eye barely notices. That is why a photo can shrink to a fraction of its raw size and still look fine. The trade-off is that every save discards a little more, so repeatedly editing and re-saving a JPG slowly degrades it.

## How PNG works

PNG compresses without losing any pixel data. Screenshots, logos, charts and text-heavy images stay crisp, and PNG supports transparent backgrounds. The cost is size: a photographic PNG is often several times larger than the same JPG.

## When to convert

- Convert **JPG to PNG** when you need to edit an image repeatedly without further loss, or place it on a design with other lossless assets.
- Convert **PNG to JPG or WebP** when a photo is too heavy for a web page.

Converting JPG to PNG will not bring back detail that was already lost. It only stops further loss. If you want to try it, the [JPG to PNG converter](/jpg-to-png) works in your browser, and the [image compressor](/image-compressor) can bring file size back down afterwards.

## Quick comparison

- **Photos on a website:** JPG or WebP
- **Logos, icons, UI screenshots:** PNG
- **Images with transparency:** PNG or WebP
- **Archiving an edit-in-progress:** PNG
